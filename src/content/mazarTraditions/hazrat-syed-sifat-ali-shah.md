In the spiritual landscape of Bengal, Abangi Tilla in Sylhet stands as a revered sanctuary deeply tied to the spread of Islam and the arrival of the saintly pioneers who reshaped the region. Elevated high above the surrounding hills, this sacred site served as the primary base and spiritual center where the great Sufi saint Hazrat Shahjalal and his 360 companions first established their presence upon arriving in Sylhet. It was from the heights of this hill that Hazrat Shahjalal proclaimed the call to prayer, the Adhan, initiating a transformative spiritual wave that brought down the reign of the tyrannical King Gour Govinda and allowed Islam to flourish throughout Bengal.

Among the key companions of Hazrat Shahjalal was his close associate and secretary, Hazrat Syed Sifat Ali Shah. Renowned for his devotion, service, and spiritual discipline, Syed Sifat Ali Shah remained intimately connected to the operations of the Sufi order and the daily life of Hazrat Shahjalal. Following the conquest, Abangi Tilla became a dedicated site for deep meditation, solitary devotion, and continuous prayer. It is recorded in regional traditions that all 360 saints gathered together on this hill in unified council, shedding tears and crying out in passionate supplication to the Divine for guidance, spiritual success, and the victory of faith in the land.

Today, the elevated peak of Abangi Tilla holds the blessed tomb of Hazrat Syed Sifat Ali Shah, after whom the hill and shrine complex are named. The sanctuary houses both his resting place and the preserved meditation retreat, or chillakhana, where the holy saints spent long periods in intense spiritual devotion and worship. Pilgrims ascend more than three hundred stairs to reach the hilltop, seeking blessing at the shrine where history, divine grace, and the enduring legacy of Hazrat Shahjalal and Syed Sifat Ali Shah converge in peaceful silence.
